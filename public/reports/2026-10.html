📌 KEY TAKEAWAY

Total traffic held steady, but its composition changed: organic search fell back to 191 sessions (-24% vs September), close to its June level, while direct rose to 452 sessions (+16%). Direct now accounts for 67% of all sessions (57% in September) and organic search for 28% (37% in September). Referral remained relatively stable at 28 sessions, while organic social (1 session), paid search (2) and AI Assistant (1) stayed marginal. With 3 newsletters and 3 LinkedIn posts in October, part of the direct increase is likely campaign-driven: UTM tagging would make it possible to confirm it and to assess the real contribution of each channel.

The audience grew but engagement deteriorated sharply: users rose by 10% to 584, yet the engagement rate fell from 31.48% to 18.32%, the bounce rate rose to 81.68% and the average session duration dropped from 1:15 to 0:43 — the weakest values on all three indicators since these reports began in November 2025. Pages per session also fell to 1.08. With 96% of users being new, a larger share of visitors now leaves after a single, short interaction: improving internal linking, content relevance and pathways towards related technical resources and service pages is the most direct lever to make better use of the traffic already acquired.

SEO Performance

Long-term trends and keyword analysis

📌 LONG-TERM TRENDS

October confirms the downward trend in search visibility observed since June: impressions fell by a further 13% to 10,595 — a fourth consecutive monthly decline that brings them 49% below their June level — and clicks dropped by 26% to 124. Both now stand at their lowest level over the whole period covered (since July 2024), and the year-on-year comparison is significant: -37% impressions and -59% clicks vs October 2025. The average position, by contrast, has hardly moved: at 12, it is identical to October 2025 and remains within the narrow 10–17 band observed since autumn 2025. ORS Labs is therefore keeping relatively stable rankings while losing search visibility — the issue is less a ranking loss than a shrinking base of searches on which the site appears. This makes it important to look beyond the average position and to investigate the evolution of the keyword portfolio, search demand and page-level visibility: rebuilding visibility around relevant technical topics should become a priority, alongside ongoing CTR optimisation.

Branded searches and specific technical queries remain the most effective sources of organic clicks, while several strategic topics generate significant impressions without translating into traffic. Rather than targeting broad terms, the priority is to strengthen the pages associated with these technical searches, improve their positioning — most of them sit on page two — and make their search snippets more compelling.

Conclusion

October shows a mixed picture for ORS Labs. Overall traffic remained relatively stable compared with September, with 677 sessions (-1%) and more users (584, +10%). However, the acquisition mix shifted towards direct traffic (+16%), while organic search declined by 24%. The three newsletters sent during the month may have contributed to the increase in direct traffic, highlighting once again the importance of UTM tracking to better understand the contribution of marketing campaigns.

User engagement deteriorated significantly. Despite the increase in users, the engagement rate fell to 18.32%, the bounce rate rose to 81.68% and the average session duration dropped to just 43 seconds. The additional visitors did not translate into deeper interactions with the website: improving internal navigation and encouraging visitors to explore related technical content and service pages should therefore remain an important objective.

From an SEO perspective, October confirms the downward trend in search visibility observed since June. Impressions fell to 10,595 and clicks to 124, both their lowest level over the period covered. Compared with October 2025, impressions are down 37% and clicks 59%, while the average position is unchanged at 12. ORS Labs is maintaining relatively stable rankings but appearing in fewer search results, which makes the recovery of search visibility a key priority.

The landing page analysis reveals contrasting results. The Humidity Conversion tool maintained its ability to attract organic clicks and improved its CTR, while the Moisture Calculator lost 38% of its clicks. Specialised technical resources such as the Materials Degassing page and the Variable Leak Rate PDF showed encouraging progress. These results reinforce the value of ORS Labs' highly specialised technical content, although further work is needed to ensure that its visibility translates into consistent traffic.

Overall, October highlights two main challenges: reversing the decline in organic search visibility and improving visitor engagement once users reach the website. The next phase should focus on strengthening the visibility of strategic technical topics, optimising key landing pages and developing stronger internal linking pathways. Continuing the PDF-to-landing-page strategy could also help ORS Labs make better use of its existing scientific expertise and attract more qualified organic traffic.
