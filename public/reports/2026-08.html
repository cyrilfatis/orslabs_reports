📌 KEY TAKEAWAY

Fewer visitors came to the site in August, but those who did were more engaged: the engagement rate gained almost 6 points to 32.81% — back to its June level — while pages per session (1.17) and session duration (1:25) remained broadly stable. With 92% of users being new and only 1.17 pages viewed per session, the structural priority remains unchanged: turning single-page visits into deeper journeys through internal linking and clearer calls to action.

Search performance continues to be driven by a combination of branded searches and highly specialised technical queries. "gross leak test" remains the main non-branded query driving clicks (2 clicks, 247 impressions, position 8.3), while "gross leak bubble tester" generates significant exposure (300 impressions) but ranks only at position 17.8, at the bottom of page two. Branded queries ("oneida research services", "ors labs", "orslabs", "ors france") generate around 700 impressions for only a handful of clicks, at average positions between 8 and 18: the site does not currently dominate its own brand SERP. IVA queries ("internal vapor analysis", "hr-iva testing", "iva testing") each generated 79 impressions with no click, at positions ranging from 13.5 to 64.5 — a topic on which the site is visible but not yet competitive.

Two levers stand out for the coming months: reworking titles and meta descriptions on the pages targeting high-impression queries, and securing the brand SERP.

Conclusion

August shows a marked slowdown across all indicators. Sessions fell by 45% compared with July, with direct traffic alone accounting for around three quarters of the decline, and organic sessions decreased by 37%. All the main acquisition channels fell at the same time, while the engagement rate improved, which points to a holiday effect on overall demand. The organic decline, however, has a cause of its own: impressions on the main landing pages stayed at their July level, so the clicks were lost to a drop in rankings rather than to a drop in demand.

The nature of the decline has also changed since July. In July, rankings were stable and the issue was reduced exposure; in August it is the opposite. The average position moved from 12 to 17 and has been deteriorating since May 2026, CTR slipped to 1.00%, and clicks fell three times as fast as impressions. Both impressions (14,592) and clicks (142) are at their lowest monthly level over the whole period covered in this report. The August 2026 spam update, rolled out between 18 and 21 August, generated significant volatility in search results over the second half of the month, but the trend started well before the summer.

Several indicators nevertheless moved in the right direction. The engagement rate improved by almost 6 points to 32.81%, impressions held up on the main landing pages, and a few pages gained ground in the rankings. The audience was smaller in August, but more engaged.

The analysis of queries and landing pages confirms two structural findings. First, organic traffic is highly concentrated: the two humidity calculator pages alone generate 64% of all organic clicks. Second, a significant share of the visibility is never converted into visits — the English home page, for instance, recorded 842 impressions for a 0.2% CTR. Closing this gap does not require new content: it requires reworking pages that already rank.

The performance of the technical PDF documents remains mixed, but several continue to generate meaningful organic visibility. This supports the existing strategy of identifying the most promising PDFs and progressively transforming them into optimized HTML landing pages, in order to make better use of existing expert content, strengthen internal linking and convert existing search visibility into additional qualified traffic.

Overall, August combines a seasonal drop in overall traffic with a loss of rankings.
